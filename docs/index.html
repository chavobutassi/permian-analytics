02 · THE EFFICIENCY

More oil, from fewer rigs.

The story of modern shale is efficiency. Longer laterals, better completions, and smarter targeting mean operators pull more barrels from each rig every year. The proof is in how two lines move relative to each other.

03 · THE ENGINE

Running hard, just to stand still.

Shale wells decline fast — a fresh well can lose more than half its output in its first year. So every month the basin must add enough new-well production to replace the natural decline of everything drilled before it. This chart takes that tension apart.

04 · THE INVENTORY

The pipeline of future barrels.

Between drilling and production sits a buffer: wells drilled but not yet completed — the DUC inventory. It is the basin's shock absorber, a reserve of near-ready supply operators bring online when prices justify it.

Why this reading matters.

Energy independence and the resilience of critical infrastructure are national priorities for the United States. Faster, more transparent, reproducible production analytics — built on the same public data operators and regulators rely on — support better decisions on resource allocation, transport capacity, and supply continuity. Benefits that reach well beyond any single company.

Every figure here is generated automatically from official data: no manual downloads, no cherry-picked windows. The same pipeline can be pointed at any U.S. basin.

Method. Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration, Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO), via the EIA API v2. Monthly, basin/region granularity; values past the latest actual month are EIA forecasts (shaded). Crude & tight oil in million bbl/day; new-well and base-change series in thousand bbl/day. Well-level decline curves, water cut, and gas-oil ratio require well-level data (Texas RRC / New Mexico OCD) and are out of scope at this granularity. Built with Python (ETL) and Plotly.
