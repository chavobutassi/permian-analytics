03 · THE ENGINE

Running hard, just to stand still.

Shale wells decline fast — a fresh well can lose more than half its output in its first year. So every month the basin must add enough new-well production to replace the natural decline of everything drilled before it. The green bars are what new wells add; the red bars are what the existing base loses. The navy line is the net — and while it stays above zero, the Permian grows. —

04 · THE INVENTORY

The pipeline of future barrels.

Between drilling and production sits a buffer: wells that are drilled but uncompleted — the DUC inventory. It is the basin's shock absorber, a reserve of near-ready supply operators can bring online quickly when prices justify it. Its rise and fall tracks the tension between how fast wells are drilled and how fast they are completed.

Why this reading matters.

Energy independence and the resilience of critical infrastructure are national priorities for the United States. Faster, more transparent, reproducible production analytics — built on the same public data operators and regulators rely on — support better decisions on resource allocation, transport capacity, and supply continuity. Benefits that reach well beyond any single company.
